Language, Society, and the Individual

Assignments developed for LIN 207 Language, Society, and the Individual, University at Buffalo, Fall 2026. Each includes step-by-step instructions and grading criteria and can be adapted for other courses.

Draw-A-Map Task

A perceptual dialectology activity. Students and a consultant each draw where they believe people speak differently on a blank map, label and explain each area with a linguistic example, then compare the two maps in a short reflection on language variation.

Learning goal: to explore how people perceive linguistic variation across geographical regions. The maps record perceptions, not an objectively accurate dialect map. The consultant can be anyone willing to share how they perceive language differences; no linguistics background is needed.

For students and instructors in sociolinguistics or introductory linguistics · PDF, 2 pages · Available · Fall 2026

New Expressions

Students write six mini-dictionary entries, three for slang expressions found online and three for expressions they create, analyzing origin, word formation, and use in context, followed by a reflection on language change, word formation, and identity. Includes an entry template.

Learning goal: to explore how new expressions emerge, how speakers create language, and why some expressions spread while others never catch on. Entries are compiled into a class mini-dictionary; students may opt out without affecting their grade.

For students and instructors in sociolinguistics, morphology, or introductory linguistics · PDF, 5 pages · Available · Fall 2026

Languages of the World

Languages in Eastern Asia

Guest lecture slides for LIN 106 Languages of the World (instructor: Gladys Camacho-Rios), University at Buffalo, October 15, 2025. Covers language families of Eastern Asia, languages in China and Chinese (Sinitic) languages, Chinese dialects, and a case study of Anyuan Hakka and the AYHAK documentation project.

About this version: these are the slides as presented in October 2025. Some slides rely on audio, video, and in-class explanation; the overview of language families and Chinese dialect groups (slides 3–24) reads best on its own. Anyuan Hakka transcriptions in the slides predate the current AYHAK conventions; see AYHAK for current forms. Maps and images from other sources belong to their creators and are not covered by this site's CC BY license.

For students and instructors in introductory linguistics or typology courses · PDF, 72 slides · Available · October 2025

Community

Community-based Language Work

Linguistics in Anyuan Hakka

In 2024, I developed an introductory course in general linguistics and taught it in Anyuan Hakka to a group of community members who had no prior linguistics background. The course provided easy access to linguistic knowledge for local community members who do not speak English (or Mandarin) and equipped them with basic linguistic tools to better understand Anyuan Hakka, their own everyday language.

For Anyuan Hakka speakers · Taught in 2024

AYHAK · Anyuan Hakka Documentation Project

Online meetups for people from Anyuan to keep speaking Anyuan Hakka, an edible-plant photo book with local names and audio (Plants of Anyuan, in progress), and other community resources for the language. Each form is recorded with the town or village it comes from rather than merged into one standard.

Community members can contribute words, photos, and local knowledge. Before anything goes online, contributors are asked whether it can be made public, whether it can be used in research, and how they want to be credited; they can ask for changes or withdrawal at any time.

Resources Directory

Resources I have used for teaching and learning about Chinese languages and language documentation. Each entry lists the provider, the languages covered, and how to access it.

Dictionary of Taiwan Hakka

The Ministry of Education (Taiwan) dictionary of six Taiwan Hakka varieties, written in traditional Chinese characters. Useful for looking up standardized Hakka character choices and pronunciations.

Ministry of Education, Taiwan · Taiwan Hakka (six varieties) · Free online

Taiwan Hakka Corpus

A corpus of Taiwan Hakka built for the Hakka Affairs Council. Useful for finding written Hakka in context.

Hakka Affairs Council, with National Chengchi University · Taiwan Hakka · Free online

Online Dictionary of Indigenous Languages

Online dictionaries of 16 Indigenous languages of Taiwan, from the Council of Indigenous Peoples.

Council of Indigenous Peoples, Taiwan · 16 Indigenous languages of Taiwan · Free online

Chinese Language Resources Protection Project Platform

Recordings and data from the national language resources project in Mainland China.

Chinese Language Resources Protection Project, Mainland China · Chinese dialects and minority languages in China · Registration requires a Mainland China phone number; access requirements may change
